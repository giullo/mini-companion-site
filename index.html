What it does

One app for the whole bench

Painting a miniature is a long job spread over weeks. Mini Companion holds the parts that are easy to lose track of — where each model got to, what you mixed, and what is still in the box.

Progress from sprue to finished

Every project moves through six states — Sprue, Assembled, Primed, Painting, Basing, Finished — and you can step it in either direction when a model goes back for a fix.

A photo log per stage

Shoot from the camera or pull from your library. Each photo is stamped with the state the model was in, so a finished mini carries its own progress gallery — grouped, full-screen, zoomable.

Your paint inventory

Pick from a bundled catalogue of over 11,000 paints across 34 brands. Search by name or manufacturer code, filter the list down, track how full each pot is, attach the exact paints you used to each project, and keep a shopping list of what is running low.

Recipes for your own mixes

Write a mix down in parts — two Waaagh! Flesh, one Screaming Skull, one medium — from paints you own or ones still in the catalogue, and share it with a friend as a picture card they can open in the app.

Painting schemes, step by step

Basecoat, shade, layer, edge highlight. Save how you paint a surface once, attach it to any project, and tick the steps off as you go.

The stash, honestly counted

Log the kits you have bought but not started, each with a picture. When one finally reaches the bench, promote it — it becomes a project on the sprue and the picture comes with it.

History and timing

Every state change is recorded automatically. The detail screen shows how long the model sat in each stage and how long it has taken overall — no timers to start, nothing to fill in.

Reminders that nag, gently

Set a threshold per state and the app tells you when a model has been sitting too long. Reminders fire at a time of day you choose, and tapping one opens straight to that project.

How it works

Add it, move it, photograph it

There is no setup and no ceremony. The app asks for a name and gets out of the way.

Start a project

Give the model a name, or promote something already sitting in your stash. It begins on the sprue with today's date.

Move it along

Tap the state control as you go. The app timestamps the change, writes it into the project's history and reschedules any reminder.

Photograph and note

Take a picture whenever it looks worth keeping. Add a note, confirm the stage, and it lands in the log under that state.

Share the finish

When the model is done, export the project as a PDF — summary, paints, timings and every photo — and send it wherever you like.

Paints & recipes

Know what you own before you buy it again

The inventory is the part of the app you will open in a shop. It ships with the catalogue already inside, so there is nothing to download and nothing to type twice.

  • 11,442 paints, 34 brands, 195 sets bundled with the app and searchable offline.
  • Search by name or product code — separators and spacing are ignored, so “XV-17” finds “XV17”.
  • Filter by brand, set or ownership, right from the list.
  • Attach paints to a project so the recipe survives the six months until you paint the second squad.
  • A shopping list gathers the pots running low and the paints your planned recipes still need.

Mixing recipes

The mix you got right once, written down

Every painter has a colour they nailed on a Sunday afternoon and could not reproduce a month later. A recipe is where that goes: a name, the ingredients in whole parts, and a swatch you dial in yourself while looking at the real thing on the palette.

  • Built from any paint in the catalogue, owned or not, in parts — “2 : 1 : 1”, the way painters actually say it.
  • Water, medium, flow improver, thinner and retarder can carry a ratio too.
  • Every recipe is checked against your pots, so it can tell you you are out of Screaming Skull before you sit down.
  • Make Pot promotes a mix into a real paint in your inventory once you have mixed a batch of it.
  • Share a recipe as a picture card, with a file attached that another Mini Companion user can open to add the mix to theirs.

The swatch is yours to set. The app never guesses a mixed colour from its ingredients — averaging hex codes models light, not paint, and it would be confidently wrong while you are looking at the real thing.

Painting schemes

How you paint it, step by step

A mix is one colour. A scheme is the whole surface: the order you put the layers down in and what goes into each. Write it once, beside your mixes in the Recipes tab, and reuse it on every squad that needs it.

  • Each step is a technique — basecoat, shade, layer, edge highlight, glaze, drybrush and more — with an optional note.
  • A step uses a paint you own, one from the catalogue, one of your mixes, or nothing at all for the varnish.
  • Attach schemes to a project and tick the steps off as you paint. Progress is kept per project, so the same scheme can be half done on one squad and untouched on the next.
  • Attaching one offers to add its paints to the project, so pot usage can be recorded against them.

Export

The finished model, as a document

Hitting Share builds a PDF of the whole project: the summary, the paints, the full state history with the photos taken during each step, the total time it took, and any notes you type at the moment of export. Then it hands it to the normal iOS share sheet.

  • Good for a commission hand-off, a club post, or your own records.
  • Message it, mail it, AirDrop it or save it to Files — whatever the share sheet offers.

Questions

Before you download

Where is my data kept?

On your devices, and in your own private iCloud. Projects, photos, paints, recipes and stash entries live in local storage on each device and sync between your iPhone, iPad and Mac through your iCloud account — there is no account to create, no server of mine in between, and I cannot see any of it. Sync can be turned off in Settings, and the app works fully offline either way.

Does it cost anything?

No. It is free, with no subscription and no in-app purchases — nothing in the app is gated behind a payment.

Is it on the App Store yet?

Not yet. It is in open beta through TestFlight, Apple's own beta distribution: install the free TestFlight app, open the beta link, and Mini Companion installs from there. Two things to know going in — beta builds expire after 90 days, so there will be the occasional update prompt, and you can send feedback straight from TestFlight by taking a screenshot inside the app. An App Store release follows once the beta has settled.

Can I add a paint that is not in the catalogue?

Paints are added from the bundled catalogue, which keeps the inventory consistent and searchable rather than a pile of near-duplicate typing. The other way a paint appears is through a recipe: write down a mix, tap Make Pot, and it joins the inventory as a custom paint with the swatch you chose.

Does a recipe mean a full painting scheme?

It can be either. The Recipes tab holds mixes — several paints and additives combined in ratios to make one colour — and schemes, the steps for painting a surface (basecoat, wash, layer, edge highlight), each with its paint or mix and a note. Attach a scheme to a project and tick the steps off as you paint.

What if I paint a model out of order?

That is expected. States move in both directions, so a finished model can go back to Painting for a touch-up and the history records both moves.

Can I turn the reminders off?

Yes, entirely, or per state. Thresholds, repeats and the time of day they fire are all set in Settings.

Is there an iPad or Android version?

iPad, yes — the same app runs on iPhone and iPad, and on Macs with Apple silicon as an iPad app, with your data kept in step through iCloud. There is no Android version.
